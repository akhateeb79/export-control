## 1. The Central Point

> A Gulf company can comply fully with its own national law and still commit a serious violation of United States or European Union export controls. Controls attach to goods, not to exporters.

This is the fact the entire product rests on, and it is poorly understood in the target market. A trading company in a free zone reasonably assumes that satisfying the local authority satisfies its obligations. It does not.

### 1.1 Four mechanisms of extraterritorial reach

- Origin follows the item. United States controls apply to US-origin goods wherever they travel and however many times they change hands. An item in a Gulf warehouse remains subject to them.
- De minimis content. Foreign-manufactured goods containing more than a threshold proportion of US-controlled content become subject to those controls, so third-country goods can be caught.
- Licence conditions. Goods received under a European licence frequently carry re-export restrictions as conditions binding on the recipient.
- Secondary designation. Sanctions authorities can designate non-US persons for dealings with designated parties, reaching Gulf entities directly.

## 2. Regime Structure by Jurisdiction

|  | Principal instrument | Authority | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| UAE | Federal Decree-Law No. 43 of 2021 | Executive Office for Control and Non-Proliferation | Consolidated single authority |
| KSA | Sector-specific instruments | Split — nuclear and radiological under the regulatory commission; military industries under the military industries authority | Fragmented by item category |
| Oman | Customs and ministry administered | Customs directorate with the commerce ministry | Lightest domestic layer of the three |

### 2.1 UAE

- The current law replaced the earlier 2007 instrument and applies across the entire territory including free zones
- Penalties extend to substantial fines for false or misleading permit information, permit cancellation, seizure of goods, suspension or dissolution of the entity, and imprisonment in defined cases
- A whistleblower reward mechanism materially raises the probability that an internal failure reaches the regulator
- The jurisdiction is under the highest external scrutiny of any transshipment corridor, which is the market condition the product addresses

### 2.2 Saudi Arabia

- Authority is divided by item category rather than consolidated, so the licensing route depends on what is being exported
- The nuclear and radiological regulator requires an end-user and end-use certificate for controlled items, with the end user, the declared use and the item recorded as separate sections of the form
- The regulator therefore treats product, end user and end use as three separate fields of inquiry, matching the platform’s assessment structure

### 2.3 Oman

- The domestic control layer is the thinnest of the three and the least publicly documented
- Extraterritorial exposure is identical, because origin-based controls do not vary by the recipient jurisdiction
- Platform value in Oman is therefore almost entirely foreign-regime compliance rather than domestic licensing support

## 3. Why the Agent Structure Holds Everywhere

All three regimes derive from the same international non-proliferation architecture. That architecture is built on precisely the separation the platform implements: a controlled goods list, a declared end user, a declared end use, and a licensing decision weighing all three.

> The Saudi certificate requirement is direct confirmation. A regulator that asks for the end user and the end use as distinct declarations is enforcing the same distinction the platform refuses to merge.

### 3.1 What varies, and where it belongs

| Varies by jurisdiction | Where it is configured |
|---|---|
| Which national control list applies | Reference data — control list versions |
| Which authority receives an application | Configuration — authority routing per item category |
| Which local documents are required | Lookups — document types by jurisdiction |
| Penalty and retention specifics | Tenant configuration |
| Whether free zones are in scope | Reference data — jurisdiction profile |

> None of this is agent logic. Supporting a new Gulf jurisdiction is configuration and reference data, not code. The Saudi split-authority routing is the only structural addition required, and it belongs in the licence determination configuration rather than in the agent prompt.

## 4. Expansion Sequencing

| Order | Market | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | UAE | Highest scrutiny, largest addressable population, consolidated authority simplifies the first build |
| 2 | Saudi Arabia | Largest economy, but requires authority routing configuration and a fragmented regime map |
| 3 | Oman | Smallest domestic layer; value proposition is purely foreign-regime, so positioning differs |
| 4 | Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait | Same extraterritorial logic; assess demand after the first three |

- Do not treat Gulf expansion as one market. The extraterritorial exposure is identical; the domestic regimes are not.
- The pitch changes by jurisdiction. In the UAE it leads with enforcement scrutiny. In Oman it leads with foreign-regime exposure the client may not know applies to them.
